The World's Smallest Countries, Ranked

Ten sovereign nations smaller than most cities — leftovers of medieval Europe and scattered Pacific atolls that survived into the modern world. Quizzes adore all of them.

Microstates are quiz magnets for a simple reason: everyone has heard of them, and almost nobody knows where they actually are. The list below covers the ten smallest by area. Notice the pattern — they come in exactly two flavors: European survivors, medieval statelets that dodged every wave of unification, and Pacific atolls whose isolation was their independence.

1
Vatican City
0.44 km² · Europe
An entire country inside the city of Rome. Created by the 1929 Lateran Treaty. It has its own bank, stamps, and about 800 residents — but no permanent citizens by birth.
2
Monaco
2.0 km² · Europe
Smaller than Central Park, yet the most densely populated country on Earth. Ruled by the Grimaldi family since 1297. Famous trap: its red-white flag is nearly identical to Indonesia's.
3
Nauru
21 km² · Pacific
The smallest island nation and the only country in the world with no official capital city. It was once one of the richest countries per capita thanks to phosphate mining.
4
Tuvalu
26 km² · Pacific
Nine coral atolls, highest point about 4.6 meters. Earns real national income licensing its internet domain — .tv — to streaming companies.
5
San Marino
61 km² · Europe
Claims to be the world's oldest republic, founded in 301 AD. Completely surrounded by Italy — one of only three countries enclaved inside another (with Vatican City and Lesotho).
6
Liechtenstein
160 km² · Europe
Wedged between Switzerland and Austria — one of only two doubly-landlocked countries on Earth (the other is Uzbekistan). Capital: Vaduz, population ~6,000.
7
Marshall Islands
181 km² · Pacific
1,200+ islands and islets across 29 atolls, including Bikini Atoll. The land is tiny but the country spans nearly 2 million km² of ocean.
8
Saint Kitts and Nevis
261 km² · Caribbean
The smallest country in the Americas, two volcanic islands in the Lesser Antilles. Capital: Basseterre.
9
Maldives
298 km² · Indian Ocean
The smallest country in Asia and the flattest country in the world — average elevation 1.5 meters. About 1,200 islands, of which only ~200 are inhabited.
10
Malta
316 km² · Mediterranean
The EU's smallest member state, south of Sicily. Its capital Valletta is the EU's smallest capital — and the whole city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The quiz questions they always appear in

  • "Smallest country in the world?" — Vatican City, always. If Vatican isn't an option, look for Monaco.
  • "Smallest country in [continent]?" — Vatican (Europe), Maldives (Asia), Nauru (Oceania... and it's also the smallest island nation overall), Seychelles (Africa), Saint Kitts and Nevis (Americas).
  • "Which country has no capital?" — Nauru.
  • "Doubly landlocked?" — Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan. Only those two.
  • "Country inside another country?" — Vatican City and San Marino (inside Italy), Lesotho (inside South Africa).
  • "Most densely populated?" — Monaco. "Least densely populated?" — Mongolia (Greenland doesn't count; it's not sovereign).

Why so small — and still countries?

The European microstates are historical accidents that became institutions. San Marino talked Napoleon out of annexing it (he reportedly admired it); Monaco and Liechtenstein survived by aligning with larger protectors while keeping sovereignty on paper; the Vatican was carved out so the Pope wouldn't be an Italian subject. The Pacific microstates went the other way: they were remote colonial outposts that gained independence in the decolonization wave of the 1960s–80s, and their tiny land conceals enormous exclusive economic zones — Kiribati controls more ocean than India controls land.

For gameplay purposes, microstates are pin-precision tests: in myGeoChallenge's City Blitz, being 200 km off on Vaduz or Valletta costs real points, so it pays to know exactly where these dots sit — Liechtenstein on the Swiss-Austrian border, Malta just below Sicily, San Marino near Italy's Adriatic coast.
