Capitals That Aren't the Biggest City — the Complete List

The single most common wrong answer in capital-city quizzes is "the famous city." Here's every major country where the capital is somewhere else — and the story of why.

Quick: what's the capital of Turkey? If you said Istanbul, you just made the most popular mistake in geography quizzing (it's Ankara). Roughly 40 countries have a capital that is not their largest or most famous city, and quiz writers love every one of them. The good news: these cities didn't end up as capitals by accident. They fall into three neat categories, and knowing the categories makes the whole list easier to remember.

Category 1: purpose-built capitals

Some countries simply built a brand-new capital from scratch — usually to move power away from a dominant coastal city or to sit between rival regions.

Brazil → Brasília (not São Paulo or Rio)
Inaugurated in 1960, planned by Oscar Niemeyer and Lúcio Costa in the shape of an airplane. Rio had been the capital for almost 200 years.
Australia → Canberra (not Sydney or Melbourne)
Sydney and Melbourne fought so hard over the capital that in 1908 Australia built a new one halfway between them.
Nigeria → Abuja (not Lagos)
Moved from coastal Lagos in 1991 to a planned city at the geographic center of the country, neutral ground between north and south.
Kazakhstan → Astana (not Almaty)
The capital moved north from Almaty in 1997. The city has been renamed repeatedly — Akmola, Astana, Nur-Sultan, and Astana again in 2022.
Myanmar → Naypyidaw (not Yangon)
Built in secret and unveiled in 2005. It has 20-lane highways and almost no traffic — one of the world's emptiest capitals.
Egypt → moving to a New Administrative Capital
Cairo remains the capital today, but government ministries have been relocating to a purpose-built city in the desert east of Cairo.

Category 2: the compromise pick

When two big cities both want the crown, countries often hand it to a smaller, quieter city — or split the job entirely.

Canada → Ottawa (not Toronto or Montreal)
Queen Victoria chose Ottawa in 1857, partly because it sat on the border between English and French Canada — and far from the US.
United States → Washington, D.C. (not New York)
New York was the first capital, but a 1790 compromise created a neutral federal district between the northern and southern states.
South Africa → three capitals at once
Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial) — the 1910 union split power among the former colonies. Johannesburg, the biggest city, got none.
Switzerland → Bern (not Zurich or Geneva)
Technically the "federal city" — Switzerland has no official capital at all, which is very Swiss.
New Zealand → Wellington (not Auckland)
Moved from Auckland in 1865 to be closer to the South Island — the world's southernmost capital of a sovereign state.
Bolivia → Sucre and La Paz
Sucre is the constitutional capital; La Paz is the seat of government. Quizzes usually accept Sucre — read the question carefully.

Category 3: the famous city just isn't it

Sometimes there's no dramatic story — the world-famous city is simply not the seat of government, and everyone gets it wrong anyway.

Turkey → Ankara (not Istanbul)
Atatürk moved the capital in 1923, away from the imperial Ottoman city to the Anatolian heartland. Istanbul is still three times bigger.
India → New Delhi (not Mumbai)
Mumbai is the financial giant, but the British moved the capital from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911.
Pakistan → Islamabad (not Karachi)
A planned city, capital since the 1960s. Karachi remains one of the largest cities on the planet.
Vietnam → Hanoi (not Ho Chi Minh City)
Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Saigon) is bigger and more famous internationally, but Hanoi has been the capital of unified Vietnam since 1976.
Morocco → Rabat (not Casablanca)
Casablanca got the movie and the port; Rabat got the government.
Tanzania → Dodoma (not Dar es Salaam)
Officially the capital since 1996, though many government offices only finished moving decades later.
China → Beijing (not Shanghai)
Shanghai is the largest city in China. Beijing has been the political center for most of the last 800 years.
United Arab Emirates → Abu Dhabi (not Dubai)
Dubai has the skyline and the airport, but Abu Dhabi is both the capital and the largest emirate by far.

How to make it stick

Don't memorize this as forty separate facts. Memorize the rule of suspicion: when a quiz asks for a capital and one option is a world-famous megacity, be suspicious — quiz writers include the famous city precisely because it's usually wrong. Then learn the list by category: the built-from-scratch group (Brasília, Canberra, Abuja, Astana, Naypyidaw), the compromise group (Ottawa, Washington, Wellington, Bern), and the "famous city isn't it" group (Ankara, New Delhi, Hanoi, Rabat, Beijing-over-Shanghai).
